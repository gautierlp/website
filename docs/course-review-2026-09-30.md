# lepoher.co against the Hyperfreelance course

Date: 2026-09-30. Site state: `main` at `ce59b1b`, identical to `origin/main` on GitHub.
Course source: the 148 transcripts in `~/projects/personal/freelance/docs/hyperfreelance/transcripts/`, read in full. Lesson numbers below are the file numbers in that folder (module/file).

Each item names what the course says, what the site does today, and the gap. Items are grouped by weight, not by page. The "known" tag means the copy review of 2026-09-29 already listed it and it is still open.

## A. The gaps that change whether a prospect books a call

1. **The share image still sells the no-code developer.** `public/assets/images/card.jpg`, the OpenGraph image every LinkedIn or Slack share shows, reads "No-code developer based in Paris, France. Building apps with .bubble since 2022." Course 2-pos/001: proof and copy must match the target; 3-prospects/031: a sloppy or wrong visual makes strangers not want to work with you. Every share of the link contradicts the hero.

2. **No client gain, anywhere.** Course 2-pos/001, 003, 005 and 3-prospects/060: state a clear, quantified value for the client, sell the transformation, not the mechanism. The site's numbers are all delivery counts (500 features, 36 tools, 200k users, Lighthouse 100). No page says what the buyer gains in time, money or risk. Even the three outcomes the course names for this market (time saved for the team, fewer errors, better margin, 2-pos/002) never appear. Known.

3. **The proof anchors on Bubble, the headline on Technical PM.** Course 2-pos/001: a social proof that does not match the target is the third positioning failure; 1-obj/017: a mismatch between claims and credibility triggers automatic doubt. Four of the six homepage review cards say "Bubble" ("top-tier Bubble expert, hire him", "mastery of Bubble.io", "with Bubble.io" twice). Ten of the thirteen apps in Track record are 2023 to 2024 Bubble builds. The reviews page carries a 2020 review about promotional videos.

4. **The three flagship case studies show a "Draft. Waits for the client's approval." banner on the live site.** `WorkCarousel` takes every use case regardless of `status`, so the Selected work cards link to pages that announce they are unapproved drafts. Course 2-pos/010: get the client's validation, then publish; 3-prospects/031: the rendering must look finished. Either publish them as approved or hide the cards until then.

5. **No problem, no cost of the problem.** Course 3-prospects/108 and 110: open with a reassurance hook, then Problem, Agitate, Solution in the second person ("here is your problem, here is what it costs you, here is how I solve it"); 2-pos/004: name the specific problem and its visible signals. The hero has one problem hint ("nobody owns end to end") and never says what that costs the founder or the operations manager, nor how they recognise it (a founder still shipping the app himself, a backlog nobody orders, a tool the IT department will not staff).

6. **Talks about me, not about you.** Course 3-prospects/057: two "you" for every "I". The hero has 10 "I/my" against 2 "you/your".

7. **No "why one person rather than the alternatives".** Course 2-pos/006 and 4-clients/116, 120: the site should pre-answer why a freelancer rather than an agency, a PM-plus-developer pair, a hire, or doing nothing (a failed hire costs 9 to 12 months; an agency puts the intern on your account; doing nothing has an opportunity cost). `positioning.md` has this argument. The site does not.

## B. Structure the course expects and the site lacks

8. **No method or process section.** Course 3-prospects/108 (share your methodology and why it differs), 4-clients/116 (a phased plan), 5-service/128 (showing the onboarding raises conversion), 3-prospects/110 (say what happens after contact so the prospect can project). The homepage has no "how I work", no first-month plan, no rituals, no "what happens after you book". Only the Pachamama page has a "How I worked" paragraph. `positioning.md` already has "What the buyer gets in the first month" written.

9. **No reassurance in the headline.** Course 3-prospects/032: the default tagline is role plus reassurance (big names, a result) so a stranger sees at once that you are qualified. The h1 is "Hi, I'm Gautier Le Poher, a Technical Product Manager. I take over your product and ship it myself." The 1M to 2M figure and Evaboot arrive in paragraph two.

10. **No client name block.** Course 3-prospects/108: name-drop, maximum client names with company type and counterpart role; 4-clients/117: group past clients by the prospect's type. Named clients on the site: Evaboot in the hero, the rest scattered in reviews and the Track record list. BETC, Schroders, Pachamama, Hublead never appear together, and nothing groups them as "B2B SaaS" against "internal applications for a group".

11. **Thirteen items in Track record, the course wants three or four.** Course 3-prospects/032: at most four experiences, the most relevant first; 1-obj/017: one to three significant case studies make you credible; 4-clients/117: no case study beats an irrelevant one. The list also contains three empty drafts (BETC, Price Writers, Protech) that build and are reachable by URL with the text "Text to come."

12. **ICP without size or buyer.** Course 2-pos/004: segment, size (revenue, headcount, age), sector optional, and the interlocutor. The site says "B2B SaaS and internal business apps" and stops. No company size, no ARR band, no "founder" or "operations director". `positioning.md` has both segments defined.

13. **No location.** Course 3-prospects/055: prospects prefer locals who understand their context; state the city. The old share image says Paris. The new site never does.

14. **No availability line.** Course 3-prospects/105 and 111 (Malt rule, adopted for the site in the copy review, decision recorded 2026-09-29): "Available for a day-rate seat on your product." Not on the page.

15. **No price signal.** Course 3-prospects/110: state the budget range up front so both sides filter early; 3-prospects/032: not in the headline, and never a high price there. The site has no rate, no range, no "from". `positioning.md` says 600. This is a choice to make, not an oversight: the course also warns that a high displayed price cuts clicks.

16. **No guarantee or risk reversal.** Course 3-prospects/060 (the pitch formula ends with a guarantee), 4-clients/119 and 2-pos/010 (satisfied or refunded on the first month when the case studies are not yet there). The site offers nothing of the kind. Relevant because all delivery proof comes from one client.

17. **Booking with no qualification.** Course 4-clients/115: three written questions before a call (your need, what you tried, why now), a phone number for the SMS reminder, and never a form as an extra step; 3-prospects/048: pair the booking link with an alternative. The email alternative is there. Whether the Cal.com page at book.lepoher.co asks the three questions and the phone number is not verified in this review.

18. **No response-time promise.** Course 3-prospects/110 and 5-service/127: one reassurance line at the CTA ("I answer the same day"). Absent.

## C. Case study pages

19. **Results are delivery facts, not before and after.** Course 2-pos/010 and 5-service/140: results show the client's metric before and after, then granular numbers. "Postgres populated at 100 percent", "CLI at v0.3.1 in production", "Agent tools merged and deployed" are actions restated as results. The interfaces page has no client-side metric at all. The no-code exit's one business result ("one additional engineer hired after the move") is buried last.

20. **No learnings section.** Course 2-pos/010 and 4-clients/117: what went wrong, what was learned, why it is better now. Only Pachamama has "What I fixed". The three flagship studies have none.

21. **No client quote on the three flagship studies.** Course 2-pos/010: a case study ideally carries the client's quotation, obtained with the validation. Evaboot and Disko Leads have one. No-code exit, interfaces and marketing site have none, and they are the pages that carry the positioning. Known, pending JB.

22. **Duration missing from two flagship titles.** Course 2-pos/010: the title is the result with the timeframe ("0 to 120 leads a month in 6 months"). "200,000 users moved off Bubble with no downtime" and "36 tools, 40 commands, four releases" have no duration in the title. The marketing site title has "in two weeks" and complies.

23. **Adjusted-figures disclosure not settled.** Course 2-pos/010: when figures are rounded or shifted for confidentiality, say so on the page. The pages show "$1M to $2M", "about 500", "200,000 users" with no line on whether they are exact and approved or rounded. Once JB approves, either statement is fine; today there is neither.

24. **No skills or tools line at the end of a study.** Course 3-prospects/110: list the skills used at the end of each case as extra reassurance. Tools appear inline in the actions. Low weight; `positioning.md` forbids keyword stuffing, so keep it to one line if added.

25. **Bullets are not scannable.** Course 3-prospects/048 and 035: bold the key phrase of each bullet, short lines, lists of aligned length. The action bullets are full sentences with no bold lead.

## D. Reviews

26. **Anonymous reviews weaken the proof.** Course 3-prospects/108: name plus company type plus role of the counterpart. Three Malt reviews show a role only ("CMO", "Co-founder", "Head of digital strategy"), Clara Ananou has no company. The course also says quantity counts, so keep them, but the named ones should lead.

27. **The 2020 video review is off-target.** Course 4-clients/117: an irrelevant proof lowers credibility. "Gautier made several promotional videos for us" sits on the reviews page next to the product work.

28. **Ask for new recommendations.** Course 3-prospects/032: request on the platform, then offer to draft the text, works within a week in 80 percent of cases. Five Contra requests from 2024-11-12 expired unanswered (docs/testimonials.md). The Pachamama, automotive group and 2026 Evaboot work have no review at all.

## E. Choices the site makes on purpose, against the course

These are not errors. They are decisions recorded in `positioning.md` that the course argues against. Listed so the divergence is conscious.

29. **Day-rate seat first.** Course 2-pos/002 and 007: on the project-tech market, sell fixed-price projects or monthly retainers, never fight the day-rate format. Decision D11 (2026-09-24) puts a day-rate seat first and fixed-price internal applications second. The site copy follows D11.

30. **B2B SaaS leads, internal applications second.** Course 2-pos/002: focus the copy on internal tools, where value is quantifiable. The site leads with Evaboot. Segment B has three anonymised app pages and one client page, and no named reference.

31. **Title "Technical Product Manager" on the site.** Course 3-prospects/108: the title must match what the client types, which holds for Malt. Decision D4 says own pages lead with the build. The h1 does both; fine, but the reassurance rule in item 9 still applies.

## F. What the site already does right

- STAR structure on every written case study, with the label-sentence headings.
- A number in most titles and in every Selected work card.
- "What I do not do" at the CTA (course 4-clients/116, positioning rule 4).
- No fixed prices, no packaged offers pitched on the page (course 2-pos/008).
- No percentages in the copy (course 3-prospects/076).
- Confidentiality by description ("a subsidiary of a large French automotive group") and blurred placeholder names with an NDA label (course 2-pos/010).
- A booking link with an email alternative (course 3-prospects/048).
- Reviews imported from two platforms, in full, on their own page (course 3-prospects/106).
- Side projects as extra reassurance (course 3-prospects/110).

## One caution from the course

1-obj/008: polishing the website before contacting prospects is "procrastination disguised as preparation". Items 1 to 7 are worth doing now because every outreach message sends the prospect to this page and to the share image. Items 8 to 28 can wait for the first calls, and the calls will tell which ones matter.
